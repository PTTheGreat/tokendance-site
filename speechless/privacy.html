Speechless: Voice Keyboard · iPhone and Mac

Privacy Policy

Applies to the Speechless app and its keyboard on iPhone, and to Speechless for Mac · Effective November 25, 2026

The short version

  • Speechless is a voice keyboard that works with the AI service you bring. You add one or more services with your own API key. When you dictate, your data leaves your iPhone: the recording goes to the service you chose for recognition, the transcript goes to the service you chose for cleanup, and the words in your dictionary go along as hints. This happens only after you agree to it for that service in the app, and only to turn what you said into text.
  • With OpenRouter, the default, every request asks for zero data retention and no training. With any other service, that service’s own terms decide; Speechless can’t set zero retention there.
  • What you type with the ABC keyboard is not stored, logged or sent.
  • I provide no AI service, run no server, and receive no audio, text, keys or usage data. There are no analytics or third‑party SDKs in the app, no tracking and no advertising, and no data is sold.

Who makes Speechless

Speechless is made by Tao Pan, an independent developer who publishes as TokenDance (the “Tony” on this site). In this policy, “I” means Tao Pan, and “Speechless” means the app and its keyboard.

What is sent, and when

You choose which services to add: OpenRouter, OpenAI, Alibaba Cloud Model Studio (Qwen), Volcengine Ark (Doubao), SiliconFlow, or any other service with OpenAI‑compatible endpoints, by its https address. Before a service is used, the app names it and its address and asks for your consent. Nothing you say goes to a service you haven’t added and agreed to. If you agree to none, voice stays off and you can still type.

Once you have agreed, each time you dictate, the following goes straight from your iPhone to the services you chose:

  • The recording of what you said, to the service you chose for recognition. It is sent when you finish, or at a pause in the middle, so you wait less. Canceling discards a recording but can’t take back a part that has already been sent.
  • The transcript, to the service you chose for cleanup.
  • The words in your dictionary, along with these, as hints for names and terms.
  • Your API key for that service, so it knows whose request it is. As with any internet connection, the service also sees your IP address. A request may also carry the app’s name.

Right after you agree to a service, the app loads its list of models and tries recognition (with one second of silence) and cleanup (with one short sample sentence) once with your API key, so you know both work. These test requests contain nothing you said. Later, when the keyboard appears, it may contact the service you chose to have a connection ready; that request carries your API key but no audio or text.

All of these connections use HTTPS.

All of this has one purpose: turning what you said into text. That means recognizing speech, catching words a model misheard, removing filler words and false starts, and fixing punctuation.

With OpenRouter

OpenRouter (OpenRouter, Inc., USA) is the default service. With it, the app lists only models that OpenRouter marks as zero‑retention. OpenRouter receives what is listed above, passes each request on to the company that runs the model, and bills you for it. With the default models, these companies receive data through OpenRouter:

The protection I require

I require of OpenRouter, and of every company it passes your data to, the same protection that this policy describes: use it to answer the request, don’t keep it afterwards, and don’t train on it. Every request that carries audio or text to OpenRouter therefore sets zdr: true (zero data retention) and data_collection: deny, whichever models you pick, so that it is only routed to endpoints that OpenRouter lists as not storing the data after processing and not training on it. If no such endpoint is available, the request fails instead of going elsewhere.

I rely on OpenRouter’s listing for this, and I can’t inspect these companies’ systems. OpenRouter itself states these exceptions:

  • It screens requests, including zero‑retention ones, for child sexual abuse material. Content flagged that way may be kept for review, reporting and preservation where the law requires it.
  • It counts some endpoints as zero‑retention that may keep requests, only to detect, investigate and prevent abuse, without training on them (“Safety Retention”). At the time of writing that is one host, Google Vertex AI. Speechless leaves it out on every request and doesn’t list models that only run there.
  • If you choose in your own OpenRouter account to have it store your prompts, that choice applies to Speechless too.

OpenRouter explains this in its zero data retention documentation. OpenRouter itself keeps request metadata (such as time, model and token counts) for billing, under its own privacy policy. These companies’ own terms and privacy policies also apply to what they process: ElevenLabs, AssemblyAI, Mistral AI.

With any other service

If you add another service and choose it for recognition or cleanup, what you dictate goes straight from your iPhone to that service, under your own account and its own terms and privacy policy. Speechless cannot set zero retention there. How long the service keeps the data, whether it uses it for training, and where it processes it are decided by its terms and your settings with it, not by Speechless. Please read them before you agree:

  • OpenAI: privacy policy
  • Alibaba Cloud Model Studio (Qwen): privacy notice (Chinese), privacy notice of the international site
  • Volcengine Ark (Doubao): platform terms, privacy policy (both Chinese)
  • SiliconFlow: privacy policy (Chinese), privacy policy of the international site
  • A service you add by its address: ask whoever runs it.

Having a second model listen to the same recording is optional with these services. If you turn it on, the recording goes to that model as well.

In the App Store’s terms

On the App Store, Speechless declares Audio Data and Other User Content as collected and linked to you, for app functionality, and not used for tracking. That is because they leave your iPhone, for the service you chose and under your own account with it. I don’t receive them.

What I receive

Nothing. I run no server for Speechless and sell no AI usage, so I receive no audio, text, keys or usage data, and no data from any of these services either. If you email me, I get your address and what you write, and use them to answer you.

What is stored on your iPhone

  • Your API keys, one per service, in the iOS Keychain, until you change or erase them. Each is sent only to the service it belongs to.
  • Settings and your dictionary, until you change or erase them.
  • The last 30 dictations, as text. Older ones drop off the list.
  • Audio only of sentences that have not been transcribed yet, so you can try again. It is deleted once they are transcribed, when they drop off the list, or when you tap Clear.
  • A technical log with times and error messages, and no dictated text, kept for 14 days. It leaves your iPhone only if you share it yourself.

Speechless doesn’t upload any of these files. If you back up your iPhone, iOS may include them in that backup, under your own arrangement with Apple.

Typing

The ABC keyboard works on your iPhone, offline and without Full Access. What you type with it is not stored, logged or sent. iOS replaces third‑party keyboards in password fields, so Speechless isn’t used there.

Allow Full Access, and the microphone

iOS gives a keyboard network access and the microphone only when you turn on Allow Full Access. Speechless needs both for voice and uses Full Access for that: to record while you dictate, to send what is listed above, and to share your settings, keys and recent dictations between the app and the keyboard. iOS warns that a keyboard with Full Access could send what you type to its developer. Speechless doesn’t. Without Full Access, you can still type.

Speechless records only while you dictate, with the system’s microphone indicator and the keyboard’s own animation showing. One recording is at most 3 minutes, and one started with a tap stops by itself after a minute without speech. Nothing listens in the background.

Withdrawing consent

You can withdraw consent at any time, for each service separately: in the app, go to AI services, choose the service and tap Withdraw consent. Nothing more is sent to that service, and you can still type. Removing a service there removes its key too. You can also turn off the microphone or Full Access for Speechless in iOS Settings.

Deleting your data

  • Clear, in the app, deletes your recent dictations and any audio kept with them.
  • Erase all data, in the app under Privacy, deletes the keys, dictionary, settings, history, audio and log, and withdraws your consent.
  • Deleting the app removes what it stored, except that iOS can keep Keychain items after an app is deleted. Use Erase all data first, so the keys are gone too. You can also delete a key in your account with the service.
  • Data already processed by a service is covered by that service’s policies. With OpenRouter, zero‑retention endpoints do not keep it. For what a service keeps about you, contact that service.

I hold no data from Speechless about you, so there is nothing for me to show, correct or delete.

Tracking, advertising and selling data

None. Speechless doesn’t track you across apps or websites, shows no ads, and doesn’t sell or share data for marketing.

Children

Speechless is not directed at children under 13. Voice needs an account with an AI service, and the services set their own age limits; OpenRouter’s terms require account holders to be adults.

Processing in other countries

The services you choose may process what you dictate outside the country you are in, where data protection rules may differ. OpenRouter and OpenAI are US companies; OpenRouter passes requests on to companies in the United States and other countries. Alibaba Cloud Model Studio, Volcengine Ark and SiliconFlow may process it in China; for Alibaba Cloud you can choose the Beijing or the Singapore region, and for SiliconFlow the China or the international service. For a service you add by its address, it depends on where that service is. If you don’t want this, don’t agree to that service in the app: typing works without any.

Speechless for Mac

The Mac app, downloaded from this site, works with the same services and the same rules: what is sent, to whom, the consent for each service, and zero retention with OpenRouter are as described above. These things are different:

  • Also sent: corrections you taught it, along with the transcript, so the cleanup model follows them: one about a word only when that word comes up again, a general note every time.
  • Accessibility. macOS asks you to allow it. Speechless uses it to notice its shortcut, to see whether the thing with the keyboard is a text field, and to paste the text there. To notice the shortcut, the system shows it key presses; it looks for the shortcut and for Esc, and doesn’t record or send any of them. It doesn’t read what is in your windows.
  • The clipboard. Text is pasted through the clipboard and stays on it, unless you turn on restoring it. Clipboard managers and Apple’s Universal Clipboard can see it there.
  • The microphone is on only while you dictate. A recording started with a tap is cancelled by itself after a minute without speech.
  • Stored on your Mac: API keys in the Keychain; settings, dictionary and corrections; the history of your dictations (the text, what was heard, the time, which app it went to, the models used and, where the service says, the cost); and the recordings of the latest ones and of any that failed, so you can try again. You can delete each entry, or everything under Settings › Privacy › Erase All Data.
  • There is no technical log, and no automatic update: the app contacts only the services you added.
